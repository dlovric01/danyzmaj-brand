DANYZMAJ · FANGED Z · DESIGN DEVELOPMENT

Concept locked.
Now the craft.

The fanged Z is the mark. Everything else is deleted. What follows is not a new set of ideas — it is the development phase: the same concept pushed along four axes, one route rejected on the record, and a refinement that takes only the decisions that survived.

A logo is not finished when the idea reads. It is finished when the idea reads and nothing in the drawing is accidental.

WHAT I WOULD FLAG IN THE CURRENT DRAWING

Three faults, named.

1 · The tail is an arrowhead

A symmetrical triangle on the bottom-right terminal reads as a direction sign, not a barbed tail. It is the one shape in the mark that points at something instead of belonging to the animal.

2 · The eye is a window

A square hole punched in a square bar reads as an aperture. An eye wants a direction and an angle; a square has neither.

3 · The weight is one notch light

At 16px the jaw closes up before the letter does. Slightly heavier strokes buy back the gape and make the favicon carry the whole idea instead of half of it.

FOUR STUDIES

One axis each, so the cause of every change is visible.

Anatomy, restraint, motion, weight. Nothing is combined at this stage — combining before you know which single change did the work is how you end up unable to explain your own logo.

08.1

Overbite

REJECTED
44·26·16
CHANGED
The head becomes its own mass, deeper than the bar, with the upper jaw overhanging a recessed lower jaw.
FIXES
The head is currently implied by a notch rather than present as a shape. Real skulls overhang.
TRADE
The mass protrudes above and below the bar, so the letter gains a stepped silhouette.

Rejected. The step reads as a drawing error before it reads as anatomy, and it costs the clean Z that made this mark worth choosing. The constraint this proves is worth keeping: the head may never break the letter’s silhouette.

08.2

Slit

KEEP
44·26·16
CHANGED
Eye becomes an angled slit. Gape deepens. Brow horn removed. Tail becomes a nicked spade.
FIXES
Faults 1 and 2. The slit gives the eye a direction; the spade stops pointing.
TRADE
Loses the horn, so the silhouette is quieter and slightly less obviously a dragon.

Keep the slit eye. It is a strictly better eye at every size, and removing the horn costs less than I expected.

08.3

Raked

KEEP
44·26·16
CHANGED
The whole mark sheared nine degrees to the right. Nothing else touched.
FIXES
Nothing structural — it addresses stance. The base mark stands still; this one leans into the bite.
TRADE
Shearing slants the terminals, so it needs its own wordmark spacing and it fights a strictly vertical layout.

Keep as an alternate, not the primary. Worth having for motion, video and anything with momentum.

08.4

Anvil

KEEP
44·26·16
CHANGED
Bar 26 to 30, diagonal 24 to 27, counters tightened. The arrow tail replaced by a hooked barb.
FIXES
Faults 1 and 3 together. The hook belongs to the animal; the extra weight holds the jaw open at 16px.
TRADE
Heavier means less air. Push further and it stops being a letter and becomes a blob.

Keep the weight and keep the hook. This is the single most valuable study of the four.

THE RECOMMENDATION

08.5 — weight and hook from Anvil, eye from Slit, silhouette from the original.

Three faults closed, nothing added. The tail is a barb that hooks back on itself instead of an arrow that points away. The eye is an angled slit with a direction. The strokes are heavier, so the jaw is still open at 16 pixels. The head stays flush with the bar, which is the one thing the rejected study proved you must not touch.

The brow horn is gone. It was doing less work than it cost, and without it the profile of the skull reads more clearly.

08.6 — the same drawing, raked nine degrees, available as the alternate for motion.

THE ONLY TEST THAT SETTLES IT

Every study at 26 and 16 pixels.

Judge a mark at the size it will actually be used, not at the size you drew it. Squint at this row: the heavier studies hold the jaw, the lighter ones close up.
