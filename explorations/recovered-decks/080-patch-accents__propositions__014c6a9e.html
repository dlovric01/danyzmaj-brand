DIRECTION 08A

Quiet Fang

the volume down

The same idea with everything decorative removed. No horn, no fang, no tongue, no barbed tail — one jaw cut into the top-left terminal and one square eye. It reads as a serious monogram, and the animal only arrives on the second look.

Removed: brow horn, both fangs, the tongue, the barbed tail. Kept: the jaw and the eye.

DIRECTION 08B

Ridgeback

the detail becomes a system

The dragon-ness moves out of the head and into the spine: three scale nicks cut into the diagonal, plus a spade tail. Because they are cut in rather than stuck on, the silhouette stays a clean Z — and the nick becomes a device you can repeat on rules, edges and dividers.

Added: three subtractive scale nicks along the diagonal, a spade tail. Removed: the brow horn, the lower fang, the tongue.

DIRECTION 08C

Twohead

the myth, structurally

The Slavic zmaj is rarely single-headed, and a Z already has 180-degree rotational symmetry — so this puts a head at each terminal. Turn the mark upside down and nothing changes. Two jaws, two eyes, two gold tongues.

Added: a second jaw, eye, fang, horn and tongue at the bottom-right terminal. The mark is now its own 180-degree rotation.

HOW TO CHOOSE

Two questions, not ten.

Who sees it most? If it is mostly clients on documents and in inboxes, take 08A. If it is mostly people online — GitHub, X, laptop lids, conference badges — keep 08.

Do you want the identity to keep giving? 08B is the only one where the logo hands you a device you will still be using on slide 40. 08C is the only one with a fact about itself worth telling out loud.

All four are the same animal and the same letter, so choosing wrong is cheap — the wordmark, the palette logic and the applications carry over.
