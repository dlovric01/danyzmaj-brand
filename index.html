How to read it

Letter first, creature second.

The top arm is the entire head — both jaws are inside that one bar, and the gape is the cut that separates them. The diagonal is the neck running into the body. The bottom arm is the tail: no teeth, no eye, and it ends in the barb.

  • Upper jaw and skull. 14.5 deep at the muzzle, widening to 17.6 at the throat, then solid 26 all the way back. Carries the eye (08) and the horn (04).
  • Lower jaw. A 3.3-deep strip at the muzzle, 6.4 at the throat. Both fangs (06, 07) hang off it. This is the thinnest thing in the mark and the first to close up at small sizes.
  • The gape (09) is the knockout between them. It stops at x 47 — behind that point the jaws rejoin and the bar is one solid piece.
  • The nub. The 5 units of gape that overshoot the muzzle do not open the mouth — they fill solid. Finding 02.

Findings · before you pick tweaks

Fixed

01 — The embers were cropped away

The three original sparks sat at x −2 → 8 while the viewBox started at x 8, so the SVG root clipped every one of them: the file rendered as solid #121212 with no accent anywhere. Replaced with a single ember at x 0.5 → 10.5 and the crop opened to viewBox="-1 0 111 100".

What that cost. The box grew from 104 × 100 to 111 × 100 — 9 units on the left, and the aspect moved from 1.04 to 1.11. Margins are now even at 3 left, 3 right, 3 top, 5 bottom. Any lockup or app-icon built on the old box needs re-centring. The diagonal's bottom-left tip, previously clipped by 0.38, is now fully inside the frame.

Measured on the current file: 1,147 orange pixels at 512 px, 32 at 96 px, 8 at 48 px, 3 at 32 px, 1 at 24 px and 1 at 16 px. It reads as a shape down to about 48 px and as a colour cue below that — but it now survives the whole ladder, which the smaller ember did not.

Breaks

02 — The muzzle overshoot fills solid instead of opening the mouth

The gape (09) runs from x 11 to x 47, but the head bar only starts at x 16. In that 5-unit overshoot the wedge is reverse-wound with nothing beneath it to subtract from, so fill-rule: nonzero counts it as filled. Verified by rasterising the path and probing: (13, 28.6), (11.8, 28.6), (13, 26) and (13, 31) all come back ink, while the same wedge inside the bar at (30, 28.6) and (46, 28.6) is correctly empty.

The result is a solid black nub floating in front of the muzzle, joined to the bar at two corner points only — and the gape behind it becomes a fully enclosed slot rather than an open mouth. That is a second unsanctioned island under DESIGN.md's hole invariant, and it is why the mouth closes up so fast at small sizes.

The real fix is to stop drawing the head as one bar with a reverse-wound wedge and draw the two jaws as separate forward-wound subpaths. Then the mouth is genuinely open at the muzzle. The cheap fix — trim the wedge to start at x 16 — removes the nub but leaves the gape a closed slot. Not touched: it changes the silhouette, and that is your call.

Drift

03 — The accent is not a brand colour

#FF6A1F is not in the danyzmaj token set. The system's stamp red is --dz-cinnabar #DA3A20; the text-safe red is --dz-oxide #8E1F0E. The whole snarl-orange series does this — worth deciding whether orange is a new token or the series should be restruck in cinnabar.

Drift

04 — The ink is not the brand ink

The mark fills #121212, a neutral black. The system ground is --dz-ink #171210, a warm iron-oxide black. On a bone sheet the difference is small; sitting on the press bed it is visible as a cool patch.

Drift

05 — Two colours inside the mark contradicts DESIGN.md

DESIGN.md read: “The mark is a single filled path with fill-rule: nonzero… Keep it that way: no second colour inside it.” Every plate in this series puts a second colour inside the silhouette. Either the rule gains an explicit accent-cut exception, or these stay explorations and the single-colour cuts remain the shipped mark.

At size

Same geometry as the file, at the sizes it has to survive.

The gape (09) is an enclosed slot, not a break in the outline — finding 02 — so it fills in early: legible at 48 px, a smudge by 24 px. The rear fang (07) and the horn's rake (04) close up around the same point, and the eye (08) is the first thing to go. The ember (10) measures 8 orange pixels at 48 px and 3 at 32 px: below 48 px it is a colour cue rather than a shape, but it now holds one pixel even at 16 px. Otherwise only the parts that touch the outline — 01, 02, 03, 05 — survive that far.

How to ask

Number + dial + direction. That is all I need.

10 — bigger, and right up against the muzzle.
06 longer, 07 gone. One fang.
02 steeper — 50° — and thinner, 20.
09 centreline up 2 — heavier lower jaw, same gape.
09 twice the gape at the mouth, same throat. Angrier.
04 three horns instead of one, stepping down toward the muzzle.
05 deeper notch, less reach. Keep the box at x 108.
08 smaller and pushed forward, toward the muzzle.
01 + 03 shallower — 22 instead of 26 — and the counters open up.

Vague is fine too: “make it snarl harder” resolves to 09, 06 and 04. Say the feeling and I will name the parts back to you before touching anything.
